# NARRATION ONLY

> Paste this block straight into your voice or avatar tool.

The Nigerian Navy E-Emolument system is where you record the personal, family and financial details that your pay is calculated from. In this short video you will see how to fill your form, save it, and submit it for approval.

Sign in with your service number and password. You land on your personal dashboard. Use the navigation bar at the top of yoour screen to navigate to the emolument screen. 

At the top you will see your name and rank. Below that is your progress tracker, showing five stages: Draft, Submitted, DO Review, FO Approval and CPO Confirm. The highlighted stage tells you exactly where your form has reached. On the left is your menu: Dashboard, My Form, Progress, History, and Ticket.

Select My Form to see a summary of what is currently on file, then open the full form to make changes. The form adjusts itself to you. If you are an officer, you will see commissioning fields. If you are a rating, you will see advancement and seniority fields instead. Trainees see their own version. You will never be asked for something that does not apply to you.

The first section is Passport Photographs. There are three: yours, your next of kin, and your alternate next of kin. Click a photo box, choose the image, and it appears immediately. Use a clear, recent passport photograph on a plain background.

Next is Personal Information — your surname and other names, service number, rank, date of birth, date of joining service, branch, specialisation, command, and ship or establishment. Then your contact and identity details: home address, state and local government of origin, religion, two phone numbers, email address, National Identification Number and marital status. Fields marked with a red asterisk are required. The form will not submit until every one of them is filled.

If you are married, record your spouse's name, phone numbers and email, and up to four children. Then your Next of Kin. You give a primary next of kin and an alternate. For each one, record the name, relationship, National Identification Number, address, phone numbers, email, and their bank account number and bank. These bank details matter — they are what the Service uses if a benefit ever has to be paid on your behalf. Check them character by character.

Under Accommodation, state your accommodation status and, where it applies, the address. Under Allowances, switch on only the allowances you are actually entitled to — for example aircrew, pilot, hazard, shift duty, rent subsidy, special forces or call duty. Do not claim an allowance you cannot support. Your Duty Officer and Finance Officer will check each one. Under Loans, record any deduction already running against your pay, with the type and the outstanding balance.

The last section is Bank and Financial — the account your salary is paid into. Account name, bank, branch and account number. Read the account number back to yourself before you move on. A single wrong digit here delays your pay.

Along the bottom of the form you have four buttons. Back returns you to your dashboard. Download PDF gives you a copy for your own records. Save Draft keeps your work without sending it anywhere. Submit Form sends it for approval. Save your draft often. If you try to leave with unsaved changes, the system will stop you and ask what you want to do. When everything is correct, press Submit Form.

Once submitted, your form locks. You can still view and download it, but you cannot edit it while it is under review. It now travels through three approvals: your Duty Officer authenticates it, your ship's Finance Officer approves it, and the Central Pay Officer confirms it. Open Progress at any time to see which stage it has reached. If something is wrong, any of the three can reject the form, and they must give a reason. Your form then reopens for you to correct — but note that it restarts the whole chain. Once you resubmit it goes back through the Duty Officer, then the Finance Officer, then the Central Pay Officer again, even if it was the last of the three who rejected it. That is why it is worth getting right the first time. Check Progress if you are waiting longer than you expect.

If you are stuck, use Ticket. Choose your issue — wrong personal data on file, a form you cannot edit, an approval status that is not updating, or something else — describe it, and submit. The administrator's reply appears under My Tickets. History keeps every form you have submitted in previous years, so you always have the record.

Fill it carefully, check your account numbers, and submit before your ship's form window closes.
